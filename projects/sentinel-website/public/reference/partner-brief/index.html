Partner Brief

A concise overview for technical buyers, operators, and program stakeholders

What it is

The Sentinel Project is the flagship trust-infrastructure product lane within a broader systems stack focused on AI-to-AI verification, policy enforcement, evidence preservation, and incident-ready reporting.

Its first product wedge is Gatekeeper: a deployable control point that monitors agent interactions, applies explicit rules at the trust boundary, and preserves qualified records for later review.

Who it is for

Sentinel is aimed at teams operating in environments where trustworthy behavior, clear controls, and reviewable records matter.

Examples include:

  • security teams
  • platform operators
  • enterprise AI programs
  • research environments
  • higher-trust or policy-sensitive deployments

What problem it addresses

As AI systems become more autonomous and interconnected, operators need better answers to basic questions:

  • What happened?
  • Which rule applied?
  • What was allowed, held, or blocked?
  • What evidence was retained?
  • Can the decision be reviewed later?

Sentinel exists to make those questions easier to answer with less ambiguity.

Core components

Gatekeeper

Monitors exchanges, evaluates them against policy, and can allow, hold, block, or escalate.

Fair Witness

Records observed facts, source context, timestamps, and uncertainty boundaries in a structured, reviewable form.

Reporting layer

Supports summaries, incident packets, and telemetry views while keeping protected evidence appropriately sealed.

Trust-material controls

Sentinel’s broader trust posture can also incorporate key provenance and lifecycle-control concepts designed to reduce the long-term usefulness of stale or compromised trust artifacts. These controls should be understood as supporting the firewall and evidence model, not replacing it.

Operating model

At a high level:

  1. activity is observed
  2. policy is evaluated
  3. a control action is taken
  4. a witness record is created
  5. evidence is preserved for review

Current maturity posture

Sentinel should be understood as a system in active development.

  • some components are live
  • some are in private alpha
  • some remain in design or lab stages

Public descriptions should stay aligned with what is actually deployed and demonstrated.

Why teams may care

Sentinel is designed to support:

  • clearer trust boundaries
  • more explainable controls
  • better evidence handling
  • more disciplined incident review
  • more credible communication with technical and policy stakeholders
  • a path toward simpler, evidence-backed trust signals as the system matures

Engagement path

Initial engagement is intake-based. We start by understanding:

  • deployment context
  • risk surface
  • policy needs
  • reporting expectations
  • review and escalation requirements
