AI DNS Trust Framework

A proposed framework for identity, trust signals, and scope validation for AI systems

AIDNS is a proposed trust framework for identifying AI systems, describing their declared scope, and tracking whether observed behavior remains consistent with that scope over time.

The comparison to internet trust infrastructure is intentional: users, operators, and procurement teams increasingly need a clearer way to ask basic questions such as who built this system, what it is allowed to do, and whether its observed behavior matches those claims.

Why this matters

  • AI systems are being deployed faster than trust frameworks are maturing.
  • Buyers and operators need clearer identity, accountability, and review mechanisms.
  • Current AI evaluation language is inconsistent across teams and vendors.
  • High-trust environments benefit from shared terminology for verification, monitoring, and review.

What AIDNS aims to provide

  • a stable identifier for a declared AI system or service
  • a record of declared scope and known capabilities
  • a trust status informed by documented review and observed behavior
  • a structured way to communicate whether a system is verified, unverified, under review, or flagged

Proposed trust states

  • Verified — declared identity reviewed, scope documented, and behavior assessed against the available criteria
  • Unverified — identity or scope not yet reviewed, or insufficient evidence for a stronger claim
  • Under review — a material question, incident, or reassessment is in progress
  • Flagged — documented issues or repeated violations require caution, restriction, or remediation

How a review model could work

Phase Purpose
Register Document system identity, operator, declared scope, and deployment context
Assess Review capabilities, boundaries, and available evidence
Monitor Track observed behavior and note material deviations
Reassess Update trust status when new evidence, incidents, or improvements emerge

Policy criteria

AIDNS is best understood as a framework for review criteria, not as a final universal standard.

Examples of review dimensions include:

  • identity disclosure
  • data-handling discipline
  • scope adherence
  • resistance to unsafe manipulation
  • escalation and reporting behavior
  • consistency over time

Current status

AIDNS is a Sentinel Project concept under active development. It should be presented as:

  • a proposed framework
  • a trust model in formation
  • a structured language for review and procurement conversations
  • a possible bridge between deep technical review and simpler future trust signals

It should not be presented as an established global authority, universal registry, or adopted industry standard unless and until that becomes true.
